# Work Package A. Economic reanalysis. Findings

Generated by `revision/code/run_econ.py`, verified by `revision/code/smoke_econ.py`.
Primary corpus 30 partitions x 6 acquisition seeds, RouterBench 20 x 6. A1--D1 at a 35% budget.
`+-` is a standard error over partitions, paired.

**No experimental result was recomputed and no reported number in the paper changed.** The committed
`results/tae_validity.json` was re-derived from scratch and matched to 0.000e+00 across 129,600
numeric fields before any of this was built.

---

## 1. The audit is cheap in absolute terms

| Corpus | Audit cost at B cells | Cells | Deployment spend it informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary, K=5 | **$2.39 +- 0.08** | 12.8 | $3,358 to $7,913 per 1M requests |
| RouterBench, K=11 | **$0.89 +- 0.02** | 26.6 | $1,347 to $2,958 per 1M requests |

Priced exactly. A readout of cell (k,j) runs model k on `n_read=30` prompts drawn from the
acquisition cohort of context j, so its price is `30 * c_acq[k,j]` with `c_acq` the mean per-request
cost of that model on that cohort. Acquisition, verification and scoring cohorts are priced
separately, because an audit dollar and a deployment dollar are not the same dollar.

## 2. What an accurate comparison is worth

The same 1-point quality tolerance applied to three different surfaces. The **decision** uses the
estimate a team actually holds; realised cost and quality are always scored on truth.

**Primary corpus**

| Decide on | Quality | $ per 1M requests | Wrong model |
|---|---|---|---|
| the log, free | 0.4794 +- 0.0039 | 3,094 +- 236 | 70.0% |
| the audited surface | 0.5057 +- 0.0023 | 5,820 +- 196 | 54.4% |
| truth, oracle | 0.5362 +- 0.0023 | 7,169 +- 330 | 7.9% |

**RouterBench**

| Decide on | Quality | $ per 1M requests | Wrong model |
|---|---|---|---|
| the log, free | 0.6884 +- 0.0067 | 1,187 +- 105 | 56.2% |
| the audited surface | 0.7257 +- 0.0034 | 1,547 +- 68 | 44.2% |
| truth, oracle | 0.7812 +- 0.0013 | 2,653 +- 40 | 8.1% |

Replicated on both corpora. The audit closes roughly a third of the wrong-model gap, which is the
paper's existing "repairs a substantial fraction, and no more" restated in the decision a team makes.

## 3. The honest negative: auditing does not reduce spend

**Every audit arm raises deployment cost.** Trusting the log costs $3,358 per 1M on the primary
corpus; CASE costs $5,891. This is not a defect in the method, it is the confounding. The router
sent easy requests to cheap models, which inflates their apparent quality, so a confounded log is
*cheap and wrong*. Correcting it moves spend toward the genuinely better models, which are the
expensive ones.

An earlier three-partition pilot suggested truth was *cheaper* than the audited surface. That did
not survive the full run and is withdrawn.

**The saving therefore does not come from auditing. It comes from acting on an accurate comparison
under a quality constraint.**

| Corpus | A 2-point tolerance saves | For a quality cost of | Break-even |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | $1,579 per 1M requests | 0.0022 | **1,514 requests** |
| RouterBench | $451 per 1M requests | 0.0021 | **1,976 requests** |

That is the budget-allocation answer. The audit costs one to three dollars and pays for itself
inside two thousand requests, but only once you use it to decide *where a cheaper model is good
enough*, which is the decision a confounded log gets wrong 56 to 70% of the time.

## 4. Cost-aware CASE. Same dollars, roughly twice the evidence

Allocation is driven by a dollar budget rather than a cell count, with priority
`w_j * u_j * (|dc| + lam*|dq|) / C_audit(k,j)`.

| Arm | Primary, cells for ~$2.4 | RouterBench, cells for ~$0.9 |
|---|---|---|
| CASE | 12.8 | 26.6 |
| **cost-aware CASE** | **23.3** | **65.8** |
| ablation, no traffic weight | 23.6 | 66.3 |
| ablation, traffic only | 19.9 | 35.2 |
| ablation, cost-denominator only | 25.5 | 67.9 |
| ablation, savings term only | 19.1 | 58.1 |

**1.8x more evidence on the primary corpus and 2.5x on RouterBench, for the same money.** The
ablations locate the active ingredient precisely: the **audit-cost denominator** does nearly all the
work, reaching 25.5 and 67.9 cells alone. Traffic weighting and the savings term contribute little
and, on their own, do worse than the full rule.

**Second honest negative.** The extra evidence does not convert into better decisions here. Quality
is 0.5060 against CASE's 0.5063 on the primary corpus and 0.7297 against 0.7285 on RouterBench, both
inside noise. Cost-aware allocation buys about twice as many cheap cells, and cheap cells are not
where the deciding margins are. This should be reported as a null result, not as a method win.

## 5. Quality-constrained cost curve

Deployment spend per 1M requests as the tolerance widens, decided on truth, primary corpus.

| Tolerance | $ per 1M |
|---|---|
| 0.000 | 6,564 |
| 0.005 | 6,564 |
| 0.010 | 6,478 |
| 0.020 | 5,830 |
| 0.050 | 2,850 |

Monotone by construction and verified as such in the smoke test. A 5-point tolerance more than
halves spend.

## 6. What is registered

Every row carries `quality`, `target_value_loss`, `wrong_model_rate`, `cost_per_request`,
`deployment_cost_per_1k`, `audit_usd`, `cells_read`, and net value at five values of gamma. The
summary adds gross and net savings at 10k, 100k and 1M requests, saving per request, break-even
requests and ROI, each with a paired standard error over partitions. Break-even is reported as
**no finite break-even** wherever the per-request saving is nonpositive, rather than as a negative
number.
